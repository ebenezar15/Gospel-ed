## The "Jesus is perfect theology" argument (summarise its logic; it is not a quotation)
- Hebrews 1:1-3: God spoke "in many portions and in many ways" through the prophets (true, inspired, partial, progressive), but finally "in his Son", who is the "exact representation" (Greek *charaktēr*, an exact imprint) of God's nature. Jesus is not a blurry picture or partial ambassador; if you want to know what God is like, look at Jesus.
- John 1:18: "No one has seen God at any time; the only begotten God… has explained him" — Greek *exēgeomai*, from which we get "exegesis". Jesus is the perfect exegesis of the Father.
- The OT is not a biography of a different, angrier God but an inspired, honest record of humanity's developing and often flawed perception of the one God who has always been like Jesus — "seeing in a mirror, dimly" (1 Cor 13:12).
- Accommodation: God condescended to work through the violent, tribal, honour-shame perceptions of a people with "hardness of heart" (Matt 19:8), met them where they were to lead them to where he is, and let his reputation be tarnished by human perception to stay in relationship. Jesus is the moment God says, "Let me show you who I have been all along."
- Answers to criticisms: (1) "You ignore the plain text of violence and wrath" → we read it with the key the text itself gives; Jesus modelled this in Matthew 5 ("You have heard that it was said… but I say to you"). (2) "This is eisegesis — reading Christ into texts" → the OT is Part One of a single story; Jesus said the Scriptures testify about him (John 5:39). (3) "This makes the OT irrelevant" → it makes it eternally relevant by showing its fulfilment; Jesus fulfils the Law and the Prophets (Matt 5:17); he is the "yes" to all God's promises (2 Cor 1:20).

## Quotations supplied by the learner (attribute to "Tommy Miller")
> The human is unknowable apart from the saving incarnation of Jesus Christ. For thousands of years, we watched ourselves bleed and guessed what a human might be. We offered sacrifices, built systems, and called our best efforts holiness. But the human remains a mystery until Jesus walks into time. He is not a superhuman; he is the human.

> The Father is unknowable apart from the saving incarnation of Jesus Christ. You can find types, shadows, poems, and dreams across the Old Testament. But the Father's face is fully revealed in the Son's face. If a reading of Scripture gives you a Father who is unlike the Son, your reading is incorrect. Scripture bears witness to the Word; it is not the Word himself (John 5:39–40). Jesus is the measure that measures all.

> Creation's destiny is unknowable apart from the saving incarnation of Jesus Christ. Genesis shows a world cascading under the weight of a confused co-regent. Creation never stopped listening to humanity; it just started hearing our fear. Only when the true human stands up does the cosmos hear its native language again—sonship. In Jesus, creation sees its next chapter.

> Now reverse all three: in Jesus, the human is fully known, the Father is fully known, and creation's future is fully known. God is not hiding behind mystery. He is unveiling in the Son.

> Tragic ontology is the attempt to use Adam's perspective to interpret Christ's redemptive acts and sees Jesus' life as exemplary rather than vicarious. What to do, rather than Who you are…. Tragic ontology endeavors to make the flesh more holy by "copying" Jesus rather than recognizing its co-death in Christ and finding life and being in union with Jesus. The reality of redemption can't be understood by the reasoning inherited by Adam through the fall.

(Bill Johnson popularised the phrase "Jesus is perfect theology".)
